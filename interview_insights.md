## Part II: The Four Personas

---

### Persona A — "The Pragmatic Early Adopter"
**Profiles:** #1, #9, #10, #13

**Who they are:** Males, 26–32, income 20–40K THB/month. Already own or are actively planning their first EV. Live in houses with home charging installed or ready to install. Research-heavy: they watch YouTube reviews, read spec sheets, compare models in spreadsheets. Car is primarily a functional tool — but it should still look respectable.

**Lifestyle needs:**
- Reliable daily transport for urban commutes of 20–80 km
- Trustworthy after-sales service they can book without waiting months
- Sufficient range for occasional inter-city trips (minimum 400 km real-world)
- Low total running cost (electricity vs. fuel calculation already done)

**Barriers:**
1. **After-sales service quality** — the single biggest pain point. Cars returned scratched, deposits not refunded, queues of months. "I had to wait 3 months for a part from China."
2. **Interior aesthetics** — black-red "gamer seat" color scheme actively rejected by multiple respondents
3. **Warranty opacity** — one respondent heard only 0.05% of battery warranty claims succeed; nobody trusts the fine print
4. **Parts supply chain** — China-origin delays create anxiety about being without a car

**Key message to convince them:**
> "BYD Thailand now has certified service centers with a written 72-hour turnaround commitment. Every technician is BYD-trained. Your battery is guaranteed for 8 years — and here's what that means in practice, with real claim examples from Thai owners. The Sealion 06 now comes in Lunar Gray interior — because we know black-red wasn't for everyone."

---

### Persona B — "The Cautious Considerer"
**Profiles:** #6, #7, #14, #16, #17

**Who they are:** Females (primarily), 28–50, income 20–50K THB/month. Safety-first, family-logistics drivers. Toyota and Japanese brand loyalists. Make decisions conservatively, often guided by family. Keep cars 7–12 years. Not technically knowledgeable — they buy based on brand trust and word-of-mouth. Current cars are mostly ICE SUVs or MPVs.

**Lifestyle needs:**
- A car the whole family can rely on without anxiety
- Practical interiors: 7-seat, easy fold-flat seats, stroller-compatible boot
- Simple ownership — charge at home and forget; no complicated routines
- Brand stability ("will this brand still exist in 10 years?")
- Visible proof of safety: crash test results, real accident outcomes

**Barriers:**
1. **Chinese brand stability fear** — news of BYD layoffs directly interpreted as "the brand is failing." One respondent rated BYD after-sales 0/10 based purely on this news.
2. **No home charger (condo/apartment)** — makes EV ownership feel impractical for day-to-day
3. **No BYD family MPV/van** — this segment wants a 7-seat large van (Zeekr 009 is on radar); BYD has no competitive product here in Thailand
4. **EV = fast-depreciating technology** — "like buying a smartphone that's already old when you get it"
5. **Battery safety incidents** — stories of batteries detaching in accidents, insurance refusing full coverage

**Key message to convince them:**
> "BYD has operated in Thailand since 2022 — here are 500 Thai families who've driven their BYD every day for 3 years, for school runs, weekend trips, and long holidays. Your Atto 3 seats 5 comfortably, fits 2 strollers in the boot, and charges overnight at home on standard power. Our battery passed Euro NCAP 5-star crash safety. And we have service centers in every major province — here's the nearest one to your home."

---

### Persona C — "The Design Connoisseur"
**Profiles:** #5, #11, #12, #19, #3

**Who they are:** Mixed gender, 26–30, income 30–70K THB/month. Aesthetics-driven buyers for whom the car is an extension of identity — "choosing a car is like choosing an outfit." They have strong opinions about design, reference European brands (BMW, Mazda, Audi) as benchmarks, and notice fine details like seat materials, dashboard color palette, and whether a design "ages well." Some are also tech-aware, but design is the gateway.

**Lifestyle needs:**
- An exterior design that feels premium and is not trend-dependent ("in 5 years will this still look good?")
- Clean, sophisticated interior — minimalist like Tesla or premium-detailed like Audi
- Neutral interior color palette: gray, black, ivory, beige — never loud colors
- A sense that owning this car signals taste, not just affordability
- Performance that feels composed and confident (no body roll, no floating sensation)

**Barriers:**
1. **BYD design not aspirational** — "rounded and Tesla-copying" (Profile #3), "slightly bulky" (Profile #19), "no beautiful BYD found" (Profile #12)
2. **Black-red + bright-color interiors are active deal-breakers** — multiple respondents in unconnected interviews named this independently
3. **BYD = affordable mass market** — this brand positioning repels aspirational buyers even if the product is good
4. **Rapid depreciation narrative** — a car that loses 40–50% in year one cannot be "premium" in their eyes
5. **No "design story"** — Profile #1 notes BYD has no generational sports car heritage (no equivalent of MX-5, Supra, GTR)

**Key message to convince them:**
> "BYD's design language — Ocean Aesthetics — was shaped by the creative directors who previously led design at Audi and Mercedes-Benz. The Seal 07's roofline is a pure coupe. The new interior palette is Ivory + Carbon Black. This is not mass-market. This is quiet luxury at an intelligent price — for people who know the difference."

---

### Persona D — "The Analytical Optimizer"
**Profiles:** #2, #15, #18, #4

**Who they are:** Male-dominant, 26–47, income 20K–400K+ THB/month (wide range). Data-driven decision makers who calculate fuel, maintenance, insurance, and depreciation before committing. Some use Excel; all make deliberate, documented choices. Research period: 3 weeks to 6+ months. They want mathematical confidence that this is the correct choice.

**Lifestyle needs:**
- Verifiable, stable total cost of ownership
- Brand pricing discipline — rapid discounts retroactively invalidate their purchase logic
- Known battery replacement cost ceiling (the single largest unknown in their model)
- Service center density and parts lead time as measurable KPIs, not vague assurances
- Financing options (installment terms, interest rates) treated equally with base price

**Barriers:**
1. **BYD's rapid price cuts break their TCO model** — "I calculated everything and then the car dropped 300K in 8 months. The math is now wrong."
2. **Battery replacement cost = unquantifiable risk** — they refuse to commit when a key variable is unknown
3. **Parts lead times from China** — unplanned downtime has a cost they can't model
4. **App/UX performance lag** — Profile #2 noted touchscreen lag as a friction point that broke his confidence in the product post-purchase
5. **Long-term resale value** — PHEV resale values are lower because battery degradation is less visible; pure EV resale is unpredictable

**Key message to convince them:**
> "Here is BYD's verified 5-year TCO: fuel savings vs. Toyota Camry Hybrid, maintenance schedule and cost, insurance benchmark, and depreciation curve. Your battery is warranted for 8 years with a maximum replacement cost cap of 150,000 THB — in writing, in your contract. Pricing on existing models is locked for 12 months post-launch. Run the numbers. We've run them for you."

## Part IV: Product-Specific Insights

### Exterior Design
- **Timeless over trendy** — Multiple respondents explicitly want a car that "ages well." Trend-heavy designs date quickly and this deters 7–10 year car owners.
- **Angular / sporty preferred over rounded** — BMW's balanced angular aesthetic is the dominant reference. BYD's rounded Tesla-influenced forms are polarizing.
- **Porsche / European visual DNA is appealing** — The Sealion 06/07 is praised specifically because it "looks like a Porsche." Communicating the European designer heritage helps.
- **Coupe-SUV crossover body is the most desirable segment** — Seal 07 is the most mentioned BYD model across design-positive respondents.
- **"Bulky" is the recurring negative** — Several respondents describe BYD as slightly bulky vs. Mazda's athletic proportions.

## Part V: BYD-Specific Strategic Recommendations

### 1. Make after-sales service a visible brand promise
Seven of nineteen respondents raised aftersales as a primary purchase veto. This is not a secondary concern — it is an outright blocker for Personas A and B. Required actions:
- Publish a written "Service Quality Charter" with measurable SLAs (response time, repair turnaround, staff certification)
- Proactively show BYD technician training at showrooms and in social content
- Implement a car-handling guarantee (if your car is damaged at our center, we pay for full restoration)

### 2. Introduce a neutral interior option prominently
The black-red interior is documented as an active sales barrier across multiple unconnected respondents. A "Lunar Gray" or "Ivory + Carbon" interior trim should be:
- A prominently marketed variant (not buried in configurator)
- The featured trim in all lifestyle photography
- Positioned as "sophisticated" rather than "basic"

### 3. Address price depreciation directly
Multiple respondents across income levels cited rapid discounting as either a reason to delay purchase (wait for it to drop more) or a quality signal (good products don't need to cut prices this fast). Recommended approach:
- Publish a "Price Stability" commitment: no discount on current models within 12 months of launch
- Alternatively, offer a Guaranteed Resale Value program (minimum buyback price at 3 years)

### 4. Demystify the battery warranty
No respondent fully trusted BYD's battery warranty; one cited a 0.05% claim success rate rumor. Required:
- Publish a plain-language, Thai-language warranty FAQ with real documented claim examples
- State explicitly: maximum out-of-pocket battery replacement cost is capped at [X] THB
- Display warranty terms at point-of-sale with an interactive Q&A tool

### 5. Communicate design heritage — not just price
BYD is perceived as "affordable mass market" which actively repels the design-driven and analytical segments. Shift positioning:
- Lead communication with design story (Wolfgang Egger–era ex-designers)
- Show Seal 07 in aspirational lifestyle contexts (not commuter contexts)
- Use the phrase "intelligent premium" or "quiet luxury" rather than "affordable EV"

### 6. Create a dedicated PHEV education campaign
PHEV is misunderstood by most respondents — either unknown (Profile #6 had never heard the term) or rejected as "two systems, double problems" (Profile #15). A simplified message:
> "Charge at home like a BEV. Refuel like a normal car on long trips. One car. One decision. No compromises."

### 7. Consider a 7-seat family MPV for the Thai market
Personas B respondents are actively shopping for a large family van (7-seat). Chinese brands are on their radar. BYD has no competitive product here in Thailand. This is an unserved demand pocket that aligns with BYD's manufacturing capabilities.
