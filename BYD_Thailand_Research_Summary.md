## 2. Interview Findings

*Based on 19 qualitative in-depth interviews across two phases.*
*Respondents: males and females, ages 26–50, income 20K–400K+ THB/month, Bangkok-based.*

---

### 2.1 After-Sales Service Quality
**Severity: Critical | Affects: All personas**

After-sales is the single most frequently cited purchase veto in the interviews. Seven of nineteen respondents raised it as a primary concern, and several who had never experienced BYD service still rejected it based on secondhand accounts. The barrier is not hypothetical — it is based on real incidents.

**Key quotes:**

> *"I had to wait 3 months for a part from China."*
> — Respondent #1, Age 26, Male, BYD Sealion owner

> *"When I wait 8 months for a part from the service center, it's like paying installments on a car I'm not using — ผ่อนรถฟรีโดยไม่ได้ใช้."*
> — Respondent #13, Age 28, Male, Honda City owner

> *"Deal breaker = service waiting times. I can tolerate around 1 week for repairs, but if it is more than 1 month, I might not consider because I have to use my vehicle daily."*
> — Respondent #13, Age 28

> *"The battery warranty claim — my friend who works in EV service says the real success rate is around only 0.05%. Brands deny claims citing technical conditions."*
> — Respondent #13, Age 28

**Alignment with survey:** Survey data confirms after-sales mentions as the top EV barrier. Both signal and lived experience agree: service quality is the decisive veto.

---

### 2.2 Charging Infrastructure & Range Anxiety
**Severity: Critical | Affects: All, especially family and provincial-travel buyers**

Even respondents with home chargers installed cite public charging infrastructure as a major anxiety on travel days. The barrier is psychological as much as physical — the fear of being stranded is more powerful than the statistical probability.

**Key quotes:**

> *"Every time I go on a trip I always worry about where I have to plan which location offers the charging station, and how long that's going to affect my time to reach my destination. Range anxiety — 9 out of 10 for sure. Yes, it would be a deal-breaker."*
> — Respondent #2, Age 26, Male, BYD Seal owner

> *"Charging stations are mostly still clustered in Bangkok. Going to the provinces, which I do often, is a major problem. I give it 8 out of 10 for concern about charging stations."*
> — Respondent #3, Age 26, Male

> *"PHEV would be perfect for families — use electricity for city driving and rely on fuel for long-distance travel when the children are asleep. I don't want to have to stop and wait to charge when the children are sleeping in the back seat."*
> — Respondent #16, Age 34, Female, Xpander owner

**Alignment with survey:** ~40% of respondents report convenient home charging. Yet interviews reveal that even this group experiences travel-day anxiety. The barrier is psychological, not just infrastructural.

---

### 2.3 Interior Design as Deal-Breaker
**Severity: Critical | Affects: Design-driven and research-heavy buyers**

BYD's black-red interior color scheme was named as an active deal-breaker by multiple unconnected respondents. This specific color scheme — not design quality overall — is the veto. BYD's mass-market brand positioning also actively repels aspirational buyers.

**Key quotes:**

> *"Not considering BYD because the interior is black-red. Hard deal-breaker."*
> — Respondent #9, Age 26, Male (Deepal REEV owner)

> *"Not for me — the bright seat colors are too loud."*
> — Respondent #17, Age 28, Female, Mazda CX-30 owner

> *"I could not find a beautiful BYD model. The exterior is rounded and Tesla-copying. I want something with angular, Porsche-like proportions."*
> — Respondent #12, Age 30, Female (Tesla Model Y user)

> *"BYD's design language feels slightly bulky. Mazda's athletic proportions are the benchmark for me."*
> — Respondent #19, Age 30, Female, Mazda owner

**Alignment with survey:** Survey groups "design" broadly. Interviews reveal the specific veto is the black-red interior — and independent respondents named it without being prompted.

---

### 2.4 Price Depreciation Erodes Trust
**Severity: Critical | Affects: Cost-calculator and design-aspirational buyers**

Rapid BYD price cuts are not perceived as good value — they are perceived as a quality signal. Multiple respondents across income levels cited BYD discounting as either a reason to delay (wait for it to drop more) or a rejection signal (good products do not need emergency discounts).

**Key quotes:**

> *"I calculated everything and then the car dropped 300,000 baht in 8 months. The math is now wrong."*
> — Respondent #2, Age 26, Male, BYD Seal owner

> *"Rapid discounts retroactively broke my purchase confidence."*
> — Respondent #18, Age 30, Male, BMW PHEV owner

> *"EV ownership feels like investing in stocks — technology changes very quickly and vehicle values fluctuate significantly."*
> — Respondent #16, Age 34, Female

**Alignment with survey:** Price/value is consistently in top-3 purchase factors. Interviews clarify the fix is pricing stability, not lower prices.

---

### 2.5 PHEV Misunderstood Across All Segments
**Severity: High | Affects: All segments, especially family and provincial-travel buyers**

Most respondents either do not know what PHEV is, conflate it with HEV, or reject it as "two systems, double problems." Only one respondent currently owns a PHEV (BMW 330e). Yet the latent demand is clear among family buyers with provincial travel needs — they simply have never been shown that PHEV solves their specific problem.

**Key quotes:**

> *"I don't know how HEV and plug-in hybrid are different. I view them as the same. I want a regular hybrid that doesn't need to be plugged in."*
> — Respondent #3, Age 26, Male (had never heard the distinction)

> *"It never crossed my mind. I only think of petrol or electric."*
> — Respondent #12, Age 30, Female

> *"PHEV is not as popular as BEV in Thailand, so I was not really fully advertised — it was not present, that's why it led to the elimination."*
> — Respondent #2, Age 26, Male, BYD Seal owner

> *"PHEV feels like a compromised product. It takes a little bit from ICE and a little bit from BEV — complexity because there are two systems."*
> — Respondent #2, Age 26, Male

> *"I chose PHEV because I calculated the numbers. The fuel saving in city driving is real. I use electricity for about 70% of my trips. The engine only runs when I need to."*
> — Respondent #18, Age 30, Male, only current PHEV owner in sample

**Alignment with survey:** PHEV has the lowest mean familiarity score. Both survey and interviews confirm the barrier is awareness, not product quality.

---

### 2.6 Chinese Brand Trust Gap
**Severity: Critical | Affects: Cautious Considerers, design-aspirational buyers**

The Chinese brand trust gap is driven not by product quality but by long-term brand survivability. Respondents fear being abandoned — not outperformed. News of BYD layoffs was directly interpreted as the brand failing and BYD service being discontinued.

**Key quotes:**

> *"Being an EV plus being a Chinese brand has practically become BYD's entire image. If PHEV has complex components and it's also Chinese, that's two doubts at once."*
> — Respondent #3, Age 26, Male (works in Japanese automotive QC)

> *"Chinese brand = unstable. I don't want to buy a car from a brand that might not exist in 10 years."*
> — Respondent #7, Age 35, Female, Toyota HEV owner

> *"I would never get BYD because BYD had layoffs. If I buy a BYD and they shut down Thai operations, who services my car?"*
> — Respondent #6, Age 50, Female, Toyota Fortuner owner

**Alignment with survey:** Survey shows BYD consideration rate is notably lower than Toyota/Honda. Interviews clarify the reason is brand survivability anxiety, not product dissatisfaction.

## 3. Executive Summary

This research combines quantitative data from 306 Thai consumers across three survey cohorts (180 general online, 82 China-administered, 44 motor show) with qualitative depth from 19 in-depth interviews. Together they map the Thai EV buyer landscape for BYD with specificity no single method could provide alone.

**The market is ready — but BYD has execution gaps, not product gaps.**

Thai consumers are broadly interested in EVs. BEV is the largest first-choice powertrain in the survey. Range and charging infrastructure anxiety, while real, are declining as barriers as charging networks expand. The true blockers are:

1. **After-sales service trust** — the single largest purchase veto, cited by 7 of 19 interview respondents. A single bad service experience — or a friend's bad experience — eliminates BYD from consideration entirely. This is addressable with written service SLAs, staff certification, and visible accountability.

2. **PHEV invisibility** — PHEV, BYD's strongest product for Thailand's infrastructure reality, is functionally unknown. Most respondents have never been shown a clear BEV vs. PHEV explanation. The family segment that would benefit most from PHEV (weekly highway trips, children asleep in the back) has never heard the proposition. This is a pure communication failure.

3. **Brand price discipline** — rapid BYD price cuts are perceived as a quality signal and retroactively break the purchase logic of analytical buyers. Price stability, resale guarantees, and transparent TCO data are more persuasive than any discount.

4. **Interior design specifics** — the black-red interior color scheme is an active, documented deal-breaker. Multiple unconnected respondents named it independently. Neutral interior options (Lunar Gray, Ivory + Carbon) must be promoted, not buried.

5. **Brand stability perception** — the Chinese brand trust gap is driven by survivability fear, not product distrust. Messaging must address "will BYD be here in 10 years?" — and answer it with Thai presence data, service network maps, and Thai family owner testimonials.

**The strategic opportunity is largest in two places:**
- **Family-stage buyers with provincial travel needs** who are the natural PHEV audience and have never been reached by PHEV communication
- **Urban HEV-to-BEV converters** who are mathematically ready to switch and need TCO clarity and service confidence to commit

## 4. Campaign Recommendations

### Campaign 1 — After-Sales Assurance Campaign

**Objective:** Remove the #1 purchase veto by making service quality a visible, public brand promise.

**Core message:** *"Every BYD service center in Thailand — 72-hour repair turnaround, in writing. Your technician is BYD-certified. Your battery is guaranteed for 8 years, with real Thai owner examples."*

**Key executions:**

- **Battery Lifetime Guarantee** — publish a plain-language, Thai-language battery warranty FAQ. State explicitly: maximum out-of-pocket battery replacement cost is capped at [X] THB. Display at point of sale. Show documented real claim examples from Thai owners.
- **Service Centers** — publish a written "Service Quality Charter" with measurable SLAs (response time, repair turnaround). Proactively show BYD technician training at showrooms and in social content. Implement a car-handling guarantee: if your car is damaged at our center, we pay for full restoration.

**Channels:** Showroom materials, YouTube owner testimonial series, Facebook community trust-building content.

---

### Campaign 2 — PHEV Education Campaign

**Objective:** Educate Thai consumers that PHEV exists, what it is, and why it solves problems BEV cannot — specifically for family buyers with provincial travel needs.

**Core message:** *"Charge at home every day. Drive anywhere on weekends. No charging stops. No compromises."*

**Sub-messages by barrier:**

- **Technology Assurance + Maintenance clarity:** *"BYD's DM-i system has one intelligent powertrain — not two separate cars. 70% of journeys are electric. The petrol engine is quietly there when you need it, not a second burden."*
  - Publish real-world battery health data after 12 months of fuel-only driving (directly addressing the "battery dies if you don't charge" fear from Interview #11)
  - Feature Respondent #18's validated real ownership experience: 70% electric, petrol only on demand

- **TCO vs. two maintenance systems:** *"BYD PHEV: one service schedule, not two. Fuel savings on daily city drives. No planning on long trips."*
  - Publish a clear 5-year TCO comparison: BYD Song DM-i vs. Toyota Fortuner HEV
  - Address the "two systems = double problems" objection with simplified system diagrams

- **Match with Thai infrastructure:** *"Thai highways don't yet have chargers on every corner. BYD PHEV was made for Thailand — electric inside Bangkok, fuel on the road to Chiang Mai."*
  - Show real Bangkok → Chiang Mai journey content: zero charging stops, family sleeping in the back

- **Target: Value of time and cost over convenience:** *"Stop calculating charging stops. Your weekend trip just got simpler."*
  - Target HEV owners (warmest audience) with: "You already believe in hybrid. Now cut your fuel bill by 60% for city driving."

- **Long-range travel → Peace of mind:** The emotional core of the PHEV message for family buyers. Children sleeping. No charging stop. Petrol refills in 3 minutes, anywhere.
  - Key visual: family in the car at night on a highway, children asleep in back seats, dashboard showing electric mode, no charging anxiety

**Channels:** Facebook (family community groups), YouTube long-form content creators, dealer demo days targeting Fortuner/Carnival/Xpander/Corolla Cross HEV owners.

**Product:** BYD Sealion 6 DM-i as the hero vehicle for this campaign.

## 5. Target Personas

### Persona 1 — First Jobbers
*(Matches Persona A: The Pragmatic Early Adopter)*

**Profile:** Male, 24–30, income 20–35K THB/month. Living in a house, home charger available or ready. First car buyer or replacing a family car. Research-heavy — YouTube, spec sheets, comparison spreadsheets. Car is a functional tool and a professional signal.

**Needs:**
- Reliable daily urban commute (20–50 km)
- Trustworthy after-sales — no months-long wait times
- Low running cost (electricity vs. fuel already calculated)
- Neutral interior color options (Lunar Gray, not black-red)

**Barriers:** After-sales quality, warranty opacity, interior aesthetics

**Message:** Service quality guarantee + transparent TCO + neutral interior options

**Channels:** YouTube (EV review channels), TikTok, online EV communities

**Products:** BYD Dolphin, Atto 3, Sealion 6

---

### Persona 2 — Family with No Kids (Couple)
*(Matches needs-based Time-Efficient Professional + Cost Calculator)*

**Profile:** Couple, 28–38, combined income 60–100K THB/month. House dwellers. Weekend travelers. Joint purchase decision. May not have children yet but lifestyle includes travel, visiting family in provinces, and weekend road trips.

**Needs:**
- No worries on weekend provincial travels
- Joint decision — both partners must feel comfortable
- Flexibility — BEV for city, fuel backup for long trips → PHEV is the answer
- No charging limitations on travel days
- Visit family outside Bangkok without range anxiety

**Message:** Peace of mind. *"Drive electric Monday to Friday. Drive anywhere Saturday and Sunday."*

**Barriers:** Charging anxiety on provincial trips, PHEV awareness gap, joint decision means one concern kills the deal

**Channels:** Facebook (couples lifestyle, travel content), YouTube travel vlog integrations

**Products:** BYD Sealion 6 DM-i, Song Plus DM-i

---

### Persona 3 — Family with Kids

**Profile:** Families with school-age children, 32–45, income 40–80K THB/month. Currently driving Japanese MPVs or 7-seat SUVs (Fortuner, Carnival, Xpander). Purchase decision is shared; children's schedule drives car requirements.

**Needs:**
- Weekend travel without charging stops (children asleep in back seat)
- Large car space — boot must fit a stroller, school bags, and weekly shopping
